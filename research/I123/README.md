# A reduction of the S-local strict LF problem

This companion concerns Theorem 5.2.1.1(1), p. 230, on the project's S-local adelic theta quotient with its original topology. The theorem remains open. The code is based on upstream commit `fb3acbf0803713d29b66a85db83f9642997ae000`. [Project issue #3916](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/3916)

**What Lean proves.** Fix a number field and a finite set of finite places. Write $K$ for the product of the local integral unit groups. Each theta class is fixed by some compact open subgroup in a single countable descending basis of $K$. These fixed subspaces are closed. For a class in a fixed subspace, averaging the finite orbit of a test representative gives a representative fixed by the same subgroup. Its finite variables form a compactly supported, locally constant function with values in the archimedean Schwartz space. [Fixed stages and representatives](FiniteStages.lean)

A general locally convex result supplies the topological step. If a Hausdorff LF-space is exhausted by ascending closed images of continuous injections from Fréchet spaces, those spaces give a strict LF presentation. The proof uses Fréchet factorization to identify the final topology and the open mapping theorem for the transition embeddings. Quotienting a parametrizing space by its closed kernel removes injectivity as a prerequisite. [Closed-image criterion](FiniteStages.lean#L242)

The remaining hypothesis is algebraic. At each open-subgroup level one must exhibit a finite family of finite-place tests whose multiplicative translates, tensored with arbitrary archimedean Schwartz functions, span all the fixed theta classes. Translates can use arbitrary finite ideles. The finite family is allowed to depend on the subgroup and must work for every class at that level. [Reduction on the exact quotient](FiniteStages.lean#L651)

The two supplied calculations address this hypothesis.

- At one finite place an invariant test is generated by the integer-ball indicator and translates of the indicator of the chosen open unit subgroup. Subtract the test's value at zero times the ball indicator. The remainder vanishes near zero and has compact support, so it occupies only finitely many multiplicative cosets. The product version gives a common finite coset expansion while the other coordinates vary, and its coefficients retain the symmetries in those coordinates. [Local and product expansions](LocalExpansion.lean#L104)
- For any fixed finite test with open stabilizer, its entire multiplicative orbit requires only finitely many tensor slices in the theta quotient. A compact set represents the norm-one S-idele classes. Its image in the discrete coset space is finite. The resulting principal translation acts on the archimedean coefficient as well, preserving the actual theta class. [Finite translation calculation](FiniteStages.lean#L455)

**Missing Lean step.** Choose the coordinate subgroups inside the given level and apply the product expansion successively to the archimedean-valued test representative. The proposed finite family consists of the product indicators obtained by choosing either the integer ball or the unit subgroup at each place. A term produced by the expansion should be the tensor of its archimedean coefficient with a finite-idele translate of one of these products. The finite-product induction and its identification with the submodule span in `strictLF_of_orbit_spans` remain to be checked. The one-coordinate expansion is uniform in all the other variables, which permits the same two choices per place for every vector at the level.
