# A proposed repair of I123 for the S-local theta quotient

Theorem 5.2.1.1(1), p. 230, issue #3916. For every number field and every finite set of finite places, the following gives a candidate proof of strict LF in the original quotient topology. The idea is to use diagonal S-units to reduce valuation indices, then use the characters of the *finite-place unit group* to split the remaining kernel. Both operations act on the actual theta quotient.

**1. Coordinates on the test space.** Write $T=S_f$, $V=\mathcal S(F_\infty)$, $K=\prod_{v\in T}\mathcal O_v^\times$, and $E=\mathcal S(\mathbf A_{F,S})$. Let $N=\ker\Phi_{F,S}$ and $q:E\to E/N$. Proposition 2.1.4.1(1), p. 71, identifies $E$ with the inductive tensor product of $V$ and the finite test space $D$. The latter has its finest locally convex topology. Thus *any* countable algebraic basis of $D$ gives $E$ the locally convex direct-sum topology on the corresponding copies of $V$. For the topology, take any family of continuous linear maps $L_i:V\to Z$ to a Hausdorff locally convex space. In coordinates $d=\sum_i d_i b_i$, the bilinear map $(v,d)\mapsto\sum_i d_i L_i(v)$ is continuous in $v$ for fixed $d$ because the sum is finite. For fixed $v$ it is continuous in $d$ because $D$ has its finest locally convex topology. The universal property of the inductive tensor product gives a continuous linear map on $E$. The inclusions of the individual copies of $V$ are continuous by the same tensor topology. These two facts give exactly the locally convex direct-sum topology. Here $V$ is the ordinary nuclear Fréchet Schwartz space.

Use the action $T_g f(x)=f(g^{-1}x)$. Every finite test is fixed by an open subgroup of $K$. Locally this follows by placing its support in a ball and taking a sufficiently small common additive period on that ball. Since $K$ is compact abelian, Fourier decomposition on the resulting finite quotient is an algebraic direct sum over smooth unit characters. There are countably many, since the congruence subgroups form a countable neighborhood basis. At a place $v$ fix a uniformizer $\pi_v$. For the trivial character take $b_j=1_{\pi_v^j\mathcal O_v}$, $j\in\mathbf Z$. An invariant test has a shell-value sequence zero far to the left and constant far to the right. Its first differences are its unique finite list of ball coefficients. For a nontrivial character $\eta$, take $b_j$ supported on $\pi_v^j\mathcal O_v^\times$, with $b_j(\pi_v^j u)=\eta(u)^{-1}$. An $\eta$-test vanishes at zero, hence in a neighborhood of zero, and compact support leaves finitely many shells. Each shell supplies one independent coefficient. Tensoring these bases gives one copy $V_{\chi,m}$ of $V$ for each smooth $K$-character $\chi$ and $m\in\mathbf Z^T$. Finite unit dilations act by $\chi$ on that entire copy.

**2. Reduce the valuations to a finite set.** Put $h=h_F$, the class number. For each $v\in T$, choose $a_v\in F^\times$ with $(a_v)=\mathfrak p_v^h$. These are S-units; their valuation vectors are $h e_v$. For $L=\langle a_v:v\in T\rangle$, valuation identifies $L$ with $h\mathbf Z^T$. This uses only a subgroup of the S-units and imposes no restriction on the ordinary global units. Choose residue vectors $R=\{0,\ldots,h-1\}^T$. For $m=r+h n$ there is a unique $\ell=\prod_v a_v^{n_v}$ taking the $r$ cell to the $m$ cell. Indeed $T_\ell$ translates each local basis index by its valuation, multiplies the ramified factors by nonzero unit-character scalars, and applies a continuous invertible dilation on $V$.

Let $W=\bigoplus_\chi W_\chi$ with $W_\chi=\bigoplus_{r\in R}V_{\chi,r}$, and let $j:W\to E$ be coordinate inclusion. Define $P:E\to W$ on cell $(\chi,m)$ by the inverse translate $T_{\ell^{-1}}$ into $(\chi,r)$, adding the contributions with the same residue vector. Each input has finite support. Each individual cell map is continuous, so $P$ is continuous by the locally convex direct-sum topology. In particular no uniform bound over $\ell$ is needed. Now $Pj=1$, and $qjP=q$ because the theta sum is unchanged by a diagonal S-unit (reindex the sum). For $N_0=j^{-1}N$, the maps induced by $j$ and $P$ are continuous inverse linear maps between $W/N_0$ and $E/N$. This identifies the **original quotient topology**. For example, over $\mathbf Q$ with finite place 2, the fold takes $\phi\otimes1_{2^j\mathbf Z_2}$ to $\phi(2^j\,\cdot)\otimes1_{\mathbf Z_2}$. It retains the real dilation that the failed projection on p. 231 drops.

**3. Split the residual kernel.** Since $\Phi(T_k f)(g)=\Phi(f)(k^{-1}g)$, $N$ is $K$-stable. The inclusion $j$ intertwines $K$, so $N_0$ is stable as well. It is closed by Theorem 5.1.1.3 and continuity of $j$. For each $w\in N_0$, choose an open subgroup $H\subset K$ in the kernels of its finitely many character components. Its $\chi$ component is the finite average $|K/H|^{-1}\sum_{k\in K/H}\chi(k)^{-1}T_k w$ for each character in its support, hence again belongs to $N_0$. Therefore $N_0=\bigoplus_\chi N_\chi$, where $N_\chi=N_0\cap W_\chi$ is closed in $W_\chi$.

The coordinate quotient maps induce a continuous linear isomorphism
$$W/N_0\;\simeq\;\bigoplus_\chi(W_\chi/N_\chi).$$
For continuity in the reverse direction, each inclusion $W_\chi\to W/N_0$ descends continuously through its quotient $W_\chi/N_\chi$; their direct sum is the inverse. Each summand is a quotient of a finite sum of nuclear Fréchet spaces by a closed subspace, hence is nuclear Fréchet. Enumerate the smooth characters, padding with zero summands if needed. Finite partial sums exhaust the right-hand side with its final locally convex topology. The coordinate projections are continuous left inverses, so these stages have their induced topologies. Each stage is also the kernel of the identity minus its finite-coordinate projection in a Hausdorff space, hence closed. This is the strict LF presentation required in #3916, including the empty finite set by a constant sequence of stages.
